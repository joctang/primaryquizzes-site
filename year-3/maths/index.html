A simple way to practise

How to use Year 3 maths practice

These short practice sets cover selected Year 3 topics, including number, fractions, money, measurement, time, space and data. They support classroom learning and do not replace a full curriculum assessment. Practise one skill at a time, explain how you chose an answer, then use the feedback to decide what to revisit.

  • For a word problem, identify what you need to find, choose an operation and check whether the answer fits the story.
  • For fractions, check that the whole is split into equal parts. The denominator names the size of the parts; the numerator counts how many parts are being considered. For example, 3/5 means 3 parts when each part is one fifth.
  • To build a whole from like fractions, keep the denominator and combine the numerators: 2/5 + 3/5 = 5/5 = 1 whole.
  • For maps and shapes, use the north arrow or compare features carefully, then read the explanation to check your reasoning.

Try a quick data investigation

With a teacher or family member, find out which of four fruits a small group prefers. Ask the same question, record each answer with a tally, then count the totals.

  1. Write the question and the four choices before you begin.
  2. Ask each person once and add one tally beside their choice.
  3. Check that the tally total matches the number of people asked.
  4. Show the counts in a table or column graph with a title and labels.
  5. Describe the most and least popular choices. Say who answered so readers know what the results represent.

Do not record names or private information. A small group survey describes only the people who answered; it does not show what every student prefers.

Make and test a number algorithm

Try this repeatable number machine, then write a new set of steps for a classmate:

  1. Choose a starting number from 1 to 10.
  2. Double it, then add the starting number once more.
  3. Record each step and the final answer. Try at least three starting numbers.
  4. Describe the pattern in the answers and explain how your partner can follow your instructions.

Example: start with 4 → double to 8 → add 4 → answer 12. What do you notice when you try 2, 5 or 8?

Use a known fact to calculate mentally

Make a friendly number, then adjust to keep the answer the same. For example, 48 + 19 = 48 + 20 − 1 = 67. For subtraction, 62 − 18 = 62 − 20 + 2 = 44.

Try 37 + 29 and 83 − 19 without writing a column calculation. Tell someone which known fact helped and how you adjusted.
