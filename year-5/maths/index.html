A simple way to practise

How to use Year 5 maths practice

These sets cover selected Year 5 skills. Read scales and labels carefully, explain how you chose a method, and keep any data conclusion connected to the group that was actually surveyed.

  • To find the mode, identify the category or value with the highest frequency, then say what it represents in context.
  • Before counting survey data, check for missing answers, duplicate entries and inconsistent labels without changing what people said.
  • Choose a display that suits the data: separate categories can be compared in a column graph; a line graph is useful for continuous change over time.
  • Try a small survey: ask classmates which lunchtime game they prefer, record one neutral response each, enter the totals in a spreadsheet, make a labelled column graph, and report the mode and who answered.
  • For a net, match each flat face to the solid, then cut out and fold a paper copy to check whether it closes without overlap.
  • Read a coordinate pair as (x, y): move horizontally first, then vertically; use the axes to locate or describe a point.
  • Describe a transformation by naming the slide, flip or turn and checking that the shape and size stay the same.
  • Choose a unit that suits the object's size: millimetres or centimetres for small lengths, metres for room-scale lengths, grams for light masses, kilograms for heavier ones, and millilitres or litres for capacity. Combine units or convert to a smaller unit when that makes a measure clearer.
  • Try a measurement station: estimate then measure a pencil in centimetres and millimetres, a bag in grams and kilograms, and a jug in millilitres. Record both the unit and the instrument reading, then explain which unit was most useful.
  • Estimate an angle by comparing it with a right angle (90°): half of one is 45°, a quarter turn is 90° and a full turn is 360°. Fold a paper corner in half to construct 45°, then check with a protractor.
  • For larger areas, think in hectares: 1 hectare = 10,000 m² (a 100 m × 100 m square). For an L-shaped block, split it into rectangles, find each area and add.
  • For duration, count on from the start time to the next hour, then add whole hours and minutes; watch trips that cross midnight.

Plan and report a small class survey

  1. Ask: “Which lunchtime game do you prefer?” Use the same neutral question for everyone and agree on the response categories first.
  2. Ask a small group once each. Record a tally without names, and check that the tally total matches the number of valid responses.
  3. Enter the categories and counts into a spreadsheet. Make a column graph with a clear title, labelled categories and an even scale.
  4. Identify the mode and describe the shape of the results. Report who answered and avoid saying the small group represents every student.

The quiz practises selected decisions; students still need to collect data, use a digital tool and communicate their own findings.
