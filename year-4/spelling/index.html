More Year 4 spelling words to practise

These extra examples extend the practice. They are not questions from the quizzes or an official ACARA word list.

Doubling a final consonant

Some short base words double the last consonant before a vowel ending. Check the word and ending together.

  • plan → planning
  • hop → hopping
  • stop → stopped
  • run → running
  • swim → swimming
  • grab → grabbed
  • skip → skipping
  • big → biggest

Prefixes and suffixes

A prefix comes before a base word; a suffix comes after it. The word family can help you remember the spelling.

  • replay
  • rewrite
  • unfair
  • misread
  • misplace
  • dislike
  • useful
  • fearless
  • sadness
  • darkness
  • payment
  • movement

More homophones

These pairs sound alike in many Australian accents but have different meanings and spellings. Use each in a sentence.

  • to / too / two
  • one / won
  • by / buy / bye
  • sea / see
  • no / know
  • new / knew
  • for / four
  • wear / where

How to practise Year 4 spelling

These sets practise common spelling patterns, word families and words that sound alike. The examples are grouped by skill so learners can focus on one pattern at a time.

  • For homophones such as their, there and they're, read the whole sentence and choose the meaning that fits.
  • When adding an ending, look at the base word first: some short words double the final consonant, as in hop → hopping.
  • Use a prefix or suffix to notice how a word is built, then check that the whole word still makes sense.
