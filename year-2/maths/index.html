How to use Year 2 maths practice

These short sets practise selected Year 2 maths skills. Use objects, drawings and explanations alongside the quizzes; a correct answer is a useful clue, not a full assessment of what a student can do.

  • When measuring with blocks or paper clips, use units that are all the same size and place them end to end with no gaps or overlaps.
  • Use the same informal unit when comparing two objects. More equal units means a longer length, greater mass or larger capacity only when the same unit is used fairly.
  • For mass, use a balance and identical counters. For capacity, use the same cup or scoop each time.
  • On a simple map, use landmarks and the direction arrow. Describe a route with steps, left/right, forward/back and turns.
  • Try a hands-on station: measure a book with equal cubes, compare two lunchboxes on a balance with identical counters, follow a route on the school map, then draw a map of a familiar room and describe a path.

Measure with equal informal units

  1. Choose same-sized blocks to measure a book. Place them end to end, with no gaps or overlaps, and count them.
  2. Compare two objects using the same kind of unit. For mass, use identical counters on a balance; for capacity, fill each container with the same cup.
  3. Try a smaller equal unit when you need to describe a remaining part more closely. Keep the unit consistent.
  4. Write what you measured and how many units it took. Explain why your comparison is fair.

Use water carefully and ask an adult or teacher before handling equipment. The quiz uses pictures and examples; hands-on measuring helps check the ideas in real situations.

Make and follow a familiar map

  1. Draw a simple map of a classroom or playground. Add a few familiar places as landmarks and mark which way is north.
  2. Start at one landmark and give a partner a route using steps and direction words such as forward, left, right, north or south.
  3. Follow your partner's directions and check whether you reach the place they named. Fix any step that was unclear.

Keep the map familiar and simple. This activity practises positions and pathways without introducing the formal grid references taught in later years.
